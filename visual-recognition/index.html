Visual recognitionas controlledmeasurement

Ziheng WuZhenghao Chen

Visual recognition uses digitally acquired evidence to estimate a query-defined property of existing physical reality. It then expresses that estimate in a shared linguistic or symbolic codebook.

When conditions or actions change what the observer can see, recognition is better modeled as a controlled measurement episode than as a fixed map from observation to text. The encoder remains necessary, but it is only one part of the observer.

Long-form research essay 13-page v3 paper 35 directly cited works
The argument in one sentence

Recognition is a physical-reality-to-report task. When acquisition determines which evidence is available, the system should expose its observation policy, evidence trace, inference, and reporting rule.

Scope

The primary claim concerns recognition. Tracking fits when the target is a communicable identity, association, location, or trajectory. Some low-level tasks fit when they report a declared image property or structured quantity. Generation and reconstruction need a different account when their objective is a new perceptual signal. A generated view remains a hypothesis until a case-linked measurement validates it.

01

Start with a room

Computer vision observes physical reality through electronic sensors and computation. Uneven illumination, occlusion, motion, limited resolution, sensor noise, and restricted access are ordinary conditions of the field. Remove them for a moment.

Put an object, scene, event, or process in an observation room. The light is adequate. Relevant surfaces are visible. The observer can move closer, refocus, or correct impaired vision. Ask one recognition question: what query-defined property should the observer identify and communicate?

The acquisition chain turns a physical state into a digital record. The observer uses that record to estimate the target and express it through a shared codebook. By suppressing acquisition failures, the room isolates a regime in which the record is stable and suitable for the target.

Definition: visual recognition in computer vision

Visual recognition uses digitally acquired evidence to estimate a query-defined property of existing physical reality. It then expresses that estimate in a shared linguistic or symbolic codebook. Consensus means that independent observers can interpret and compare reports under the same task rule. It does not require identical wording.

This definition fixes the output contract, not the architecture. The modeling claim is narrower. When conditions or actions change what the observer can see, recognition becomes a controlled measurement process with four auditable operations: observation formation, evidence acquisition, target inference, and reporting.

Let S be a physical world state and X a query. An electronic measurement operator produces the initial model-visible observation O0 ~ M0(· | S). The observation may be a raster, video, depth map, multiview collection, 3-D point set or volume, or another sampled visual structure. Encoding it as a vector does not make raw Euclidean distance a semantic metric.

A case may retain a resource R, such as an original-resolution file, a video archive, a multiview capture, or a sensor buffer not yet exposed to the model. O0 is what the model initially sees. R is what an authorized action may still reveal. The exposure contract, not the modality name, determines the boundary.

The ideal room is a control, not a canonical image or preprocessing target. Ideal-observer analysis also specifies an observation model and its information limits rather than one perfect view [1]. Many traces can preserve the evidence required by a task. No system must reconstruct the room.

02

What a one-shot map hides

A standard recognizer has the contract Z = fθ(O0, X). Training may use millions of examples, and inference may contain many internal steps. The contract remains fixed-observation when each case begins from a preselected evidence source and the system cannot choose another one.

This map often absorbs acquisition tolerance, evidence access, target inference, and reporting. The coupling has produced strong systems. AlexNet's label-preserving translations, reflections, and RGB-intensity perturbations are one example [2]. They encode target-relative invariance across digital records. They are training variants, not an episode trace.

Information limit

The distinction is absent

If target-distinct states induce the same fixed observation, no decoder can recover their case-specific difference from that observation.

Structural burden

The choice is implicit

A passive contract can fold search, comparison, correction, and memory into one input-to-report relation without representing how to expose more evidence.

Verification aliasing

The answer hides its basis

A correct final answer does not show whether the visible evidence supported it, another measurement was needed, or a tool result changed the decision.

Coupling does not make the target intrinsically unmeasurable. It can make the source of failure unidentifiable under the training contract. Acquisition may erase a distinction, the system may omit an available action, or inference may misread accessible evidence.

The alternative is a sufficient trace, not a canonical image. For task loss ℓ and tolerance ε, a trace is sufficient when its best attainable conditional decision risk is at most ε. The fixed observation may already be sufficient. It may be recoverable through a legal action within budget. Or it may remain irrecoverable under every allowed policy.

Too broad End-to-end coupling makes recognition unmeasurable.
Precise Coupling can hide whether the fixed observation identifies the target and whether another legal measurement could make it recoverable.
03

Learning to read, learning where to look

The controlled-measurement view connects two older strands of recognition research. This is a retrospective comparison. Language-based visual agents did not invent active observation.

Representation and reporting

Handcrafted pipelines faced limited CPU, memory, and storage. SIFT and HOG retained task-relevant local structure under those constraints [3][4]. Convolutional networks learned more of the compression and tolerance from data [5][6]. Self-supervised learning and vision Transformers made encoders more reusable [7][8]. Vision-language pretraining expanded the reporting interface [9][10].

These systems usually begin each inference episode from a supplied record. Video makes the record larger and adds temporal order, motion, indexing, and memory, but more frames alone do not create an observation policy. TimeSformer, for example, encodes a preselected sequence of frame patches [11]. If the complete video belongs to O0, attending to its frames is internal computation. Observation control begins when the system can expose an interval from retained material, revisit discarded evidence, request a new stream, or decide when acquisition should stop.

Observation control

Active perception and active vision treated sensing as a controlled process decades before current agents [12][13]. Animate vision coordinated perception with action [14]. Recurrent attention learned where to sample within an image [15]. Interactive and embodied perception linked action to information acquisition [16][17]. Controlled sensing and POMDPs supplied related decision machinery [18][19].

Human vision shows that learned representation and controlled sampling can coexist. Ventral representations tolerate useful appearance changes [20], while gaze depends on task and expected information [21][22][23][24]. This evidence does not imply that the brain implements the paper's formal contract.

Language-model post-training supplies a separate analogy. DeepSeek-R1-Zero used rule-based rewards and reinforcement learning without an SFT cold start; on verifiable tasks, its outputs developed longer reflection and exploration [25]. That result does not prove that visual observation policies will emerge the same way. It motivates a test: with typed actions, evidence-linked verification, cost, and stopping rules, can a recognition policy learn when and how to acquire evidence?

Current visual agents can maintain a query and memory, choose tools, inspect returned evidence, stop, and report. Existing systems already perform bounded region selection and visual operations over retained resources [26][27]. Embodied systems can also change the physical measurement. The components now coexist, but reliable joint training, calibration, provenance, authority, and safe intervention remain open.

04

The observer is the unit

For controlled recognition, the system boundary includes everything that acquires, retains, interprets, and reports evidence. The observer contains a measurement interface, provenance-bearing memory, an observation policy, a target estimator, and a reporter. A verifier sits outside it and audits the declared contract.

Observation contract
C = (M0, R, {Ma}, A, B)

The contract declares the initial channel, retained case material, action-conditioned measurement operators, legal action set, and budget.

The world and task are S, X, and Y = q(S, X). The realized trace is Ht = (O0, (ai, Oi, pi, ci)i=1..t), where each step records its action, returned observation, provenance, and cost. The policy selects the next legal action from the current trace and remaining budget. It does not rewrite the contract.

1 Form An electronic channel samples a world state into a digital observation.
→
2 Acquire A legal action exposes retained evidence or obtains a new measurement.
→
3 Infer An estimator maps the stopped trace and query to a target estimate.
→
4 Report A reporter expresses the estimate in the shared codebook.

A one-shot recognizer is the special case with no evidence-changing action and an immediate report. A controlled observer can act again, ask for authority, report, or abstain. These functions may share every parameter. Their conceptual separation matters because the trace makes each failure auditable.

05

Three quantities must remain separate

The earlier language-distance intuition combines distinct questions. V3 separates target inference, report fidelity, and evidence recovery.

01 · Inference

Did the estimate identify the target?

The estimator produces Ŷ = d(HT, X) and incurs target-inference loss ℓinf(Ŷ, Y; X).

02 · Reporting

Did the report express the estimate?

The reporter produces Z = ρ(Ŷ, X). Reporting fidelity compares Z with acceptable expressions of Ŷ, not directly with world truth.

03 · Recovery

What risk is attainable within budget?

The risk-cost frontier measures the best target loss that any legal policy and decoder can attain under the declared budget.

Reporting fidelity
Drep(Z; Ŷ, X) = infZ′ ∈ ZŶ(X) dcode(Z, Z′)

A fluent report can faithfully express a wrong estimate. Reporting fidelity therefore cannot replace inference correctness.

Evidence recovery
r*A(B) = infπ,d: c(HT) ≤ B E[ℓ(d(HT, X), Y)]

A good policy can find the right evidence while a weak encoder misreads it. Language distance does not decide whether the target was observable.

06

Actions need signatures, not names

The same interface name can hide different measurement semantics. A crop may rearrange pixels already visible, reveal an original-resolution region, or steer a sensor toward a new part of the world. The action record must say which one occurred.

Pixel attention

Current record

Select an ROI, crop, zoom, or transform pixels already exposed to the model.

Instance attention

Retained evidence

Select frames, stored instances, search results, or memory from retained case material.

World attention

New measurement

Redirect a sensor, change exposure, move closer, manipulate the scene, or capture another view.

Every evidence action declares
at = (operator, capability, provenance, preconditions, authority, target-preservation rule, trace-dependent cost)

Stop, report, abstain, and request-authority are terminal decisions rather than evidence actions. Planning is policy computation, although its cost still counts.

Provenance distinguishes post-processing of the current record, access to retained but unexposed information, a new world measurement, a measurement after intervention, and an unverified synthetic hypothesis. Post-processing may help a finite model read existing evidence, but it cannot separate physical states already identical in the parent record [28]. A generated completion can guide the next action. It does not become case-linked evidence until a measurement validates it.

Cost is a vector before the task turns it into a score. Planning, visual computation, memory, storage, bandwidth, time, energy, acquisition difficulty, physical risk, and human effort have no natural shared unit. A benchmark may constrain dimensions, publish a Pareto frontier, or declare a scalarization. It should not treat every tool call as equivalent. This action view follows established work on active recognition and sensor selection [29].

07

The hard boundary is contract-relative

Under a restricted contract, irreversible downsampling, sensor clipping, or an occluded event without an archive can leave distinct world states indistinguishable. The claim is relative to the declared observer. A different sensor, retained resource, budget, or authority may move the boundary.

Fix a query and observation contract C. Let Π(C) be the common set of legal non-anticipating policies, including stopping rules, whose stopped traces remain within budget. For world state s, let Psπ be the distribution of the stopped trace before the report.

Controlled indistinguishability

Two states are equivalent under the contract when every legal policy produces the same stopped-trace distribution in both states:

s0 ~C s1 ⇔ Ps0π = Ps1π for every π ∈ Π(C)

For two equally likely states under binary loss, the best attainable error is e*C = (1 - ΔC) / 2, where ΔC is the greatest total-variation separation produced by a legal policy.

One matching image or trace does not prove equivalence. Different states can produce the same sample by chance. The boundary is equality across every allowed policy, not resemblance between two final frames.

The full episode matters. Two episodes may end in similar pixels yet differ in acquisition path, provenance, unused authority, timing, and cost. A summary can replace the trace for target inference only when it preserves the relevant conditional information. An audit record has stricter obligations because it must also preserve how the evidence was obtained.

A larger encoder may close an accessibility gap. It cannot manufacture a case-specific distinction absent from every legal trace.

08

Alignment needs measurement episodes

Answer-only supervision checks agreement with a target. It cannot establish which observation supported a claim, whether another measurement was needed, or whether a returned artifact changed the decision.

Recent systems expose this gap. A model may answer before a requested crop, reach the right answer through a wrong visual interpretation, or learn a visual operation without using its result [30][31][27][32]. Typed actions and preserved traces do not remove semantic ambiguity. They make part of the learning signal auditable.

Measurement episode
E = (S, X, Y, C, HT, U, Z, Prov, V, I)

The record contains the world and task, observation contract, stopped trace, exposed linguistic or symbolic trajectory, terminal report, provenance, privileged verifier information, and permitted evidence interventions. It does not require private chain-of-thought.

  1. Correctness

    Are exposed semantic outputs correct relative to their referents and the task rule?

  2. Support

    Did the observation prefix available at the time support each case-specific claim?

  3. Policy

    Did the system obey the action contract, and where does its risk and cost lie?

The time index is essential. For a claim emitted at time t(c), support is judged against Ht(c), not against evidence collected later. Later evidence may confirm the claim. It cannot show that the claim was supported when it was made.

Provenance identifies the source of an artifact, not whether the decision used it. A support audit can remove or replace decisive evidence and test whether the observer seeks another measurement, changes its report, lowers confidence, or abstains. This is a diagnostic of evidence dependence, not a general proof of causal sufficiency.

The audits can disagree. A lucky answer can pass correctness and fail support. A supported answer can waste acquisitions. A policy audit must also distinguish calibrated abstention after reasonable recovery from premature abstention while a low-cost measurement remains.

09

A research program for controlled observation

The framework earns its cost only if it produces cleaner diagnoses, better calibrated policies, or more efficient learning under controlled comparison. V3 organizes that program into five layers.

P1

Observation problem. Declare the world, query, target, allowed observations, retained resources, and reporting rule.

P2

Measurement substrate. Describe sensors, archives, processors, memory, people, and actuators by provenance, authority, and cost.

P3

Boundary and policy. Measure observational equivalence and decisions to acquire, stop, report, request authority, or abstain.

P4

Long-horizon cognition. Preserve enough provenance for later reinspection without treating a fluent summary as permanent evidence. Ego4D provides one relevant episodic-memory setting [33].

P5

Discovery loop. Propose distinctions and measurements that make competing explanations predict different observations. Generated hypotheses become useful through prospective tests, not by becoming evidence themselves [34][35].

The immediate experiments are concrete. Under matched data, compute, and authority, compare measurement-episode training with strong fixed-input and end-to-end baselines. Test whether a provenance-aware evaluator separates supported from unsupported success better than terminal-text reward. Measure whether explicit answer, continue, stop, request, and abstain decisions improve the risk-cost frontier.

These are hypotheses, not theorems. Explicit modularity may fail to beat a well-designed end-to-end system. Provenance may be expensive. Some tasks may already be served by a static encoder. The additional machinery must justify itself in controlled comparisons.

10

The controlled observer

The v3 model

A recognition system receives a query about a target property of a world state. Under a declared observation contract, it builds a provenance-bearing trace. It reports when that trace supports the target, continues when a legal action is worth its cost, requests authority when needed, and abstains when the allowed measurements cannot decide.

The encoder determines what a finite model can extract from acquired evidence. The observation policy determines which evidence is acquired or exposed within the contract. The reporter makes the estimate communicable. The trace connects these operations and makes their limits auditable.

A fixed recognizer asks: given this observation, what target does it support?

A controlled observer asks a larger question: given this target, trace, authority, and budget, what should be observed next, and when is the evidence enough to report?

The ideal room was never a destination. It isolated a task-relative regime in which the input preserves target-relevant evidence and supports the report. The research program is to build observers that detect when this condition holds, seek evidence when it does not, and report when their legal measurements cannot decide.
